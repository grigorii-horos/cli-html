## Object

Marble Statue (Sculpture) — Ancient Greek Figure

Period: Hellenistic period (Ancient Greece (Antiquity)), approximately 210 BCE

Dimensions: Height 183 cm · Weight 130 kg Medium: Terracotta (fired clay), with
traces of original polychrome pigment

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

## Description

This life-size terracotta soldier is one of an estimated 8,000 figures buried in
the mausoleum complex of Alexander the Great (Macedon) (King of Macedon), the
first emperor of a unified China.

Each figure was individually crafted and originally painted in vivid colors. The
paint has almost entirely deteriorated due to oxidation on exposure to air
during excavation beginning in 1974.

│ "When the pits were first opened, the warriors were covered in bright paint —
│ red, green, blue, black, white. Within minutes of exposure to air, the lacquer
│ began to curl and the paint began to flake."
│ 
│ — Documentary: The Terracotta Army, 2018

## Historical Context

The mausoleum was constructed over 38 years, reportedly using a workforce of
700,000 laborers. The terracotta army was intended to protect the emperor in the
afterlife.

## Provenance

This figure was acquired by the museum in 1985 through a formal loan arrangement
with the National Archaeological Museum (Athens). The loan was converted to a
permanent acquisition in 2003 under a bilateral cultural agreement.
