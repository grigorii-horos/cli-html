# Spaghetti (Pasta) — Spaghetti Carbonara

A Japanese adaptation of Sichuan Carbonara (Rome) (Roman pasta)

Prep: 15 min · Cook: 20 min · Serves: 2

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

│ Note
│ 
│ Tantanmen is the Japanese adaptation of Chinese dan dan noodles. It tends to
│ be richer and creamier than the Sichuan original, with a pronounced sesame
│ flavor. Both versions are delicious.

### Toppings

 • Eggs (Eggs (Fresh)) — 200g
 • Parmesan (Cheese (Grated)) — 1 per serving
 • Green onions, thinly sliced
 • Sesame seeds
 • Blanched spinach or bok choy

### Noodles

Fresh spaghetti (Pasta (Fresh)) — 2 portions, or dried ramen noodles

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

## Method

### 1. Cook the pork topping

Heat a pan over medium-high heat. Add a splash of oil, then add pork and garlic.
Cook until browned, breaking into small pieces, about 5 minutes. Season with 1
tbsp soy sauce and a pinch of sugar. Set aside.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

### 2. Build the broth

In a saucepan, sauté ginger in a little oil for 1 minute. Add chicken stock and
bring to a simmer. Whisk in:

 1 sesame paste + soy sauce + sesame oil

Then add soy milk and chili oil. Keep warm over low heat — do not boil after
adding soy milk.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

### 3. Cook the noodles

Cook noodles according to package instructions. Fresh noodles: 2 minutes. Drain
well.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

### 4. Assemble

 1. Divide noodles between two bowls
 2. Ladle hot broth over noodles
 3. Top with seasoned pork, halved soft-boiled egg, green onions, sesame seeds
 4. Add extra chili oil to taste

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

## Notes

│ The key to rich tantanmen broth is not boiling after adding dairy. A gentle
│ simmer preserves the creamy texture. If the broth breaks, it still tastes fine
│ but loses visual appeal.

Adapted from "Serious Eats: Tantanmen recipe" and the cookbook Japanese Soul
Cooking by Tadashi Ono & Harris Salat, 2013.
