# Typography Glossary

A reference guide to typographic terms, with pronunciation guides and historical
context.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## A

Ascender ::

  The portion of a lowercase letter that extends above the x-height. Found in
  letters b, d, f, h, i, k, l, t. "Ascenders help distinguish lowercase
  letters from capitals."

Aperture ::

  The partially enclosed, somewhat rounded negative space in some characters,
  such as n, C, S, and the lower part of e. First described in typographic
  literature around the 1680s.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## B

Baseline ::

  The imaginary line on which most letters and other characters sit.
  Descenders (g, j, p, q, y) dip below the baseline. Fundamental to Typography
  (Letterpress) (movable type) systems.

Bezier curve ♪[Pronunciation: Bezier] ::

  Named after engineer Pierre Bezier (pjɛʁ bezje) (1910–1999), who popularized
  its use in CAD (Computer-Aided Design) systems. Used in PostScript and
  OpenType to define glyph outlines.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## K

Kerning ♪[Pronunciation: Kerning] ::

  Adjustment of spacing between specific pairs of characters to achieve
  visually consistent spacing. Differs from tracking, which adjusts spacing
  uniformly across a range of text. "Kerning is the fine-tuning of space
  between individual character pairs."

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## L

Leading ♪[Pronunciation: Leading (rhymes with heading, not feeding)] ::

  Line spacing — the distance from one baseline to the next. Named after the
  strips of lead used in hot-metal typesetting to increase space between lines
  of type, practiced since the mid-1400s when Johannes Gutenberg (ˈjoːhanəs
  ˈɡuːtənbɛʁk) introduced movable type to Europe.

Ligature ::

  A glyph representing two or more letters joined together. Common examples:
  fi, fl, ff, ffi, ffl. In Arabic script (العربية (al-Arabiyya)), most
  characters connect to adjacent letters.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## S

Serif ::

  Small decorative strokes at the ends of the main strokes of a letter.
  Typefaces with serifs (Times New Roman, Georgia) are called serif typefaces.
  Those without (Arial, Helvetica) are called sans-serif (sans (from French:
  without)).

Stem ::

  The main vertical stroke of a letter. In letters like I, H, l, the entire
  character may be considered a stem. Stem weight determines the apparent
  boldness of a typeface. "The stem is the skeleton of the letterform."

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## X

x-height ::

  The height of lowercase letters, specifically the letter x, which has no
  ascender or descender. A large x-height (relative to cap height) improves
  legibility at small sizes. First systematically measured in 1932 as part of
  legibility research.
