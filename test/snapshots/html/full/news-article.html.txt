# Researchers Achieve Human-Level Accuracy in Low-Resource Language Translation



By Sarah Chen and Marcos Oliveira
Published June 10, 2026, 09:30 UTC · Updated 14:15 UTC



━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A team of researchers at the ICLI (International Computational Linguistics
Institute) announced today a significant breakthrough in machine translation for
low-resource languages — those with limited training data — achieving accuracy
scores comparable to professional human translators for the first time.

▶[Press conference: ICLI researchers present translation results]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

## The Research

The new approach, called Cross-Lingual Transfer with Minimal Supervision (CTMS),
was tested on 47 languages including nehiyawewin (Plains Cree), isiZulu (Zulu),
and Nuosu Yi (Southwest China).

Lead researcher Sarah Jenkins (S. Jenkins) stated: "We trained the model on
fewer than 5,000 sentence pairs per language — a fraction of what was previously
considered the minimum for useful neural translation. The results exceeded our
expectations for every language in the test set."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

## Implications

The breakthrough has significant implications for the estimated 3,000 languages
that currently have no digital translation support. Of the world's 7,000+
languages, fewer than 100 are well-supported by existing translation services.

│ "Approximately 40% of languages are at risk of disappearing by the end of the
│ century. Digital tools that preserve and enable communication in these
│ languages are critically needed."

## Expert Reaction

Professor Muller (ˈmʏlɐ) of computational linguistics at ETH Zurich called the
result "a genuine step change, not an incremental improvement. The field has
been searching for a solution to the low-resource problem for a decade."

The paper, Cross-Lingual Transfer with Minimal Supervision for Endangered
Language Preservation, was published in Nature Language Processing on June 10,
2026. A preprint has been available since May 28.
